## What changes on 1 March 2027 for existing Victorian homes

Under Victoria's electrification standards for buildings, from **1 March 2027**, an existing mains gas water heater (including a gas pool heater) that reaches the end of its service life and needs replacing cannot be replaced with another mains gas water heater. Instead, households must install an electric water heater—such as a heat pump or solar-boosted water heater—unless a regulatory exemption applies.

Key boundaries confirmed by Energy Victoria and the Department of Transport and Planning:

- **Working gas units do not need early removal:** You only need to switch when your existing gas water heater breaks down and needs replacement.
- **Repairs are still allowed:** If your existing gas water heater can be safely repaired, you can repair it.
- **Owner-occupier gas heating and cooking are not affected:** Owner-occupiers are not required to remove or replace gas space heaters or gas cooktops.
- **Bottled LPG is not affected:** The Victoria Gas Substitution Roadmap confirms that homes using bottled LPG (rather than reticulated mains gas) are not affected by this rule.

## Exemptions to the 1 March 2027 rule (verified vs unverified)

### Confirmed in Victoria Government Gazette No. S 654 (2025)

The Building and Plumbing Amendment Regulations 2025 notice published in the Victoria Government Gazette (<SourceLink id="vic-gazette-s654-2025" label="GG2025S654" />) confirms exemptions where:

1. **Major switchboard alteration required:** Installing an electric water heater would require a main switchboard alteration or replacement.
2. **No electricity supply required:** The replacement water heater does not require an electricity supply to operate.
3. **In-warranty replacement:** The existing gas water heater is being replaced under a manufacturer's warranty.

<Callout type="caution" title="Additional exemptions cited in third-party summaries (unverified against reg 45E text)">
  Industry summaries of Plumbing Regulations regulation 45E also reference exemptions for insufficient physical space for a storage tank, centralised hot water systems in multi-dwelling apartment buildings, electrical distribution network upgrade requirements, conflicts with other laws, and contracts signed before 1 March 2027. We mark those additional items as <strong>unverified</strong> on this page until checked directly against the consolidated regulation on legislation.vic.gov.au. Your licensed plumber and accredited provider can confirm whether an exemption applies to your property.

## Rules for rental properties from 1 March 2027

Rental properties face broader minimum efficiency standards from **1 March 2027**:

- **Broken, unrepairable water heater:** Must be replaced with an energy-efficient electric water heater (heat pump or electric-boosted solar).
- **Broken, unrepairable main living area heater:** Must be replaced with an energy-efficient reverse-cycle air conditioner.
- **Start of a new lease:** Efficient cooling must be installed in the main living area when a new lease starts from 1 March 2027.
