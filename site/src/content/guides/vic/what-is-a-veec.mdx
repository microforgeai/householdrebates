## One certificate equals one tonne of emissions saved

Under the Victorian Energy Upgrades (VEU) program, each **Victorian Energy Efficiency Certificate (VEEC)** represents **one tonne of greenhouse gas emissions reduction** (`CO2-e`) estimated over the lifetime of an approved energy-saving upgrade (<SourceLink id="esc-veecs" label="Essential Services Commission — Victorian Energy Efficiency Certificates (VEECs)" />).

Households do not create or trade VEECs themselves. Instead:

1. You engage an **accredited person** (commonly called a VEU accredited provider) or a licensed installer working with one **before** your upgrade starts—see <a href="/how-quotes-work/">how VEU quotes and installers work</a>.
2. When you assign the right to create certificates to that accredited provider, they reduce your upfront invoice by an agreed point-of-sale discount (<SourceLink id="energyvic-about-veu" label="Energy Victoria — About Victorian Energy Upgrades" />).
3. After installation and compliance checks, the accredited provider registers the VEECs with the Essential Services Commission and sells them to **energy retailers**, who must buy and surrender certificates each year to meet Victoria's annual emissions targets.

## Why your VEU discount is not a fixed cash rebate

Unlike a fixed-dollar government voucher—compare <a href="/victoria/veu-vs-solar-homes/">how VEU differs from the Solar Homes program</a>—a VEU discount depends on four variables (<SourceLink id="energyvic-insulation" label="Energy Victoria — Insulation discounts" />; <SourceLink id="energyvic-window-glazing" label="Energy Victoria — Window glazing discounts" />):

- **Product efficiency and baseline:** Replacing an inefficient electric resistance or gas appliance saves more lifetime emissions (and creates more VEECs) than replacing an already efficient unit—see the scenario tables on our <a href="/victoria/air-conditioner-rebate/">Air Conditioner Rebate Victoria guide</a>.
- **Location:** Metropolitan and regional Victoria have slightly different climate baselines in the VEU specifications.
- **Provider administration costs:** Accredited providers deduct compliance, auditing, and administration costs before passing the certificate value onto your quote, and you must always pay at least <a href="/victoria/veu-minimum-co-payment/">the mandatory VEU minimum co-payment</a>.
- **Certificate market value:** Energy Victoria's indicative product pages illustrate how certificate assumptions work—for example, Energy Victoria states that its indicative induction cooktop discount is based on <Money id="vic-veu-induction-cert-price-basis" /> per certificate (<SourceLink id="energyvic-induction" label="Energy Victoria — Induction cooktop discounts" />), which is the certificate price Energy Victoria based its induction figure on rather than a live market quote.

We never publish trader certificate quotes because there is no official Victorian Government live price feed; your accredited provider confirms the exact dollar discount in your written quote.

## Upcoming rename to "energy upgrade certificates" (Act No. 37/2026)

Under Victorian legislation passed in **Act No. 37/2026**, the Victorian Energy Efficiency Target Act (VEET Act) will be renamed and **VEECs** will be renamed **energy upgrade certificates** **on proclamation** (<SourceLink id="esc-veu-legislation" label="Essential Services Commission — VEU program legislation" />). Until proclamation takes effect, official forms, quotes, and Essential Services Commission registers continue to refer to VEECs, and household eligibility works the same way—check your scenario in the <a href="/victoria/eligibility-checker/">Victoria Rebate Eligibility Checker</a>.
